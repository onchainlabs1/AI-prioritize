What This Workbench Helps You Decide

Purpose

The goal is not to create more process. The goal is to help teams make better portfolio decisions with a shared view of:

  • which ideas look strong enough to move now;
  • which ones deserve planning next;
  • which ones still need clearer scope, ownership, or evidence;
  • which ones should be reframed before more time is spent.

What Drives The Recommendation

Model
Business value

How meaningful the expected impact is for revenue, cost, risk, customer experience, or operations.

Time-to-value

How quickly the initiative can create useful results and whether the timing actually matters.

Feasibility

How realistic delivery looks based on data, systems, operating model, and implementation effort.

Confidence

How strong the evidence is behind the recommendation, so weaker assumptions are visible instead of hidden.

How To Read The Outcome

Interpretation
Tier A: move now

The initiative looks strong enough to move with urgency and clear momentum.

Tier B: plan next

The case is promising, but the best next step is planning, shaping, or targeted discovery.

Tier C: incubate

The idea may still matter later, but it is not yet clear or strong enough for commitment.

Not a fit now

The idea should be reframed, paused, or stopped before more portfolio time is invested.

What Happens Behind The Scenes

Built in
  • the tool applies a best-fit route for the type of initiative being assessed;
  • simple readiness checks surface blockers before more effort is spent;
  • weighted scoring keeps comparisons consistent across initiatives;
  • evidence quality adjusts confidence so assumptions stay visible;
  • decision history stays attached to the initiative for future review.

What Teams Get

Outputs
  • a clear recommendation and lane for each initiative
  • a comparable score across opportunities
  • visible rationale instead of ad-hoc judgment
  • a record of decisions and follow-up direction
  • shared language for business, product, and architecture teams

Designed To Stay Lightweight

Principles
  • defaults first, detail only when needed
  • fast comparison before deep analysis
  • decision support, not decision theater
  • enough structure to stay consistent without slowing teams down
